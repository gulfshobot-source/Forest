## Core tensions to preserve

- universal yet not empty/generic
- highly structured yet naturally simple
- recursive without becoming a rigid Russian-doll hierarchy
- dependency-aware without assuming one directional stack
- portable without being defined by a vendor/protocol/domain
- emergent without losing governability or provenance
- visually digestible without reducing underlying depth
- persistent without freezing evolving meaning
- compositional without assuming a fixed global alphabet
- locally cellular while still participating in larger-scale organization

## Working experiment questions

1. What is a “cell” at the smallest useful scale, and can it itself be a collection?
2. What is a “character” in the user’s phrase “catalog of characters” without prematurely reducing it to a type or schema?
3. Does a collection determine its possible characters, do characters generate the collection, or are they mutually constitutive?
4. Which properties recur across scale, and which transform?
5. Is there a meaningful largest collection, or only context-dependent horizons?
6. Can dependencies be local, recursive, cyclic, overlapping, and scale-relative while remaining navigable?
7. What information must remain invariant for something to be portable across representations, models, systems, and domains?
8. Can visualizations be active projections/participants rather than static illustrations while canonical truth remains elsewhere?
9. What mathematical formalisms illuminate these questions without forcing the concept into their assumptions?
10. What should remain permanently unresolved or plural rather than standardized?

## Promotion rule

No idea from this experiment should enter the canonical Forest merely because it is elegant.

Promote only when it either:

- explains a persistent user-originated signal better than existing Forest architecture;
- produces a useful capability or representation that survives testing;
- resolves a known architectural tension without erasing useful alternatives; or
- reveals a reusable law that improves multiple Forest subsystems.
